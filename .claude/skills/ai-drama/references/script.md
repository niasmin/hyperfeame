# Writing the episode

## Shape of a 2-minute episode (12 shots)

| Shots | Job |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hook**: the most emotional frame of the episode, mid-moment (a slap, a confession, tears, a reveal). No setup. |
| 2–3 | Rewind or context: who these people are, what they want, in action, not explanation |
| 4–8 | Escalation: each shot raises the stakes or turns the situation; one turn per shot |
| 9–10 | The truth or the choice arrives |
| 11 | Reaction: the face that has to live with it |
| 12 | **Cliffhanger**: a new question (a message arrives, a door opens, someone was listening). Cut before the answer. |

A series keeps one question open across episodes and answers a smaller one in each.

## Dialogue rules

- Spoken Thai, the way people really talk: particles (นะ, ค่ะ, ครับ, อะ, เหรอ), short sentences, no narration-style lines.
- One line per shot; if a character needs more, give them another shot (often a reverse angle).
- Subtext beats exposition: "เธอยังใส่แหวนวงนั้นอยู่อีกเหรอ" tells more than explaining the backstory.
- Every line moves the story or reveals character; cut lines that only describe what the picture already shows.

## Genres that work in short vertical drama

Revenge, hidden identity (rich heir, CEO in disguise), betrayal and second chances, office romance, family secrets, time-slip or period drama, school romance. Pick one engine per series and keep the tone consistent.

## Before handing the script on

Read the 12 lines alone: does a stranger follow the story? Look at the 12 actions alone: do they tell the same story without sound? If not, rewrite before any prompt is written.
